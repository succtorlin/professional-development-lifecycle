# PD Essential Education Practice

## R — 原文 (Reading)

> “What do I want my students to learn and be able to do? What evidence would I accept to verify that learning?”
>
> — Guskey & Jung, *Answers to Essential Questions...*, Chapter 2, pp. 25–27

> “Teachers use their classroom assessments as learning tools, both to provide students with feedback and to guide the correction of learning errors.”
>
> — Guskey & Jung, Chapter 7, pp. 44–47

> “The first and most important step in implementing standards-based grading and reporting is to define its purpose.”
>
> — Guskey & Jung, Chapter 25, pp. 108–110

## I — 方法论骨架 (Interpretation)

Start with the learning goal, not with a label, test, gradebook, or report-card template.
Separate what students should learn from what they should be able to do and how performance will be judged.
Name the decision an assessment must support: improve learning, diagnose, report, certify, place, or evaluate a program.
Choose evidence that represents the target; a test is only one possible source.
When evidence is collected during learning, use it immediately for feedback, correction, enrichment, and reassessment.
For team improvement, teachers jointly develop, score, and analyze common formative assessments.
Before changing grades or reporting, define what grades mean, what evidence counts, who needs the information, and how it will be used.
Standards-based reporting is a communication and implementation redesign, not a cosmetic report-card change.
For exceptional or struggling learners, classify an adaptation by what it does to the measured target: access support is an accommodation; a changed target is a modification.
Always state the local policy, legal, measurement, equity, and accessibility limits of the recommendation.

## A1 — 应用示例 (Application Example)

### 示例 1: Common formative assessment team

- **类型与来源**: 原书转述/工作示例；Chapter 8, pp. 48–51。
- **问题/输入**: A teacher team shares a standard and needs to know which concepts students have mastered across classes.
- **方法论的使用**: The team aligns a common assessment to the standard, administers it at about the same time, scores consistently, tallies item/criterion errors by class, shares successful strategies, and plans corrective activities.
- **结论**: All classes’ results can reveal whether a problem is class-specific or shared across instruction.
- **结果**: 原书报告了项目分析与教学回应逻辑；不把它当成普遍成效估计。

### 示例 2: 合成演练（原书未提供统一端到端案例）

- **问题/输入**: A school wants a standards-based report card for a Grade 6 science unit and asks whether a student’s support changes the grade basis.
- **方法论的使用**: Write the content, observable scientific behavior, and performance criteria; decide whether the assessment is for learning or of learning; use formative evidence for correction; define grades as evidence of the standard; classify extra time as an accommodation if the target is unchanged, or as a modification if the target changes; report the basis in plain language.
- **结论**: The deliverable is an aligned learning/evidence/reporting design with a documented adaptation classification—not simply a new letter-grade scale.
- **结果**: This is a rule-based walkthrough, not evidence about the fictional school.

## E — 可执行步骤 (Execution)

**必需输入**: learning standard/goal; subject and learner context; decision and audience; available instructional time and evidence; grading/reporting purpose; for inclusive cases, current support plan and local policy. Ask for missing goals, purpose, learner context, or legal/policy authority instead of guessing.

**交付物与完成标准**: aligned goal statement; assessment-purpose/evidence map; formative response plan or team cycle; grading-purpose charter; reporting/communication plan; adaptation classification and grade-basis record where relevant; explicit limitations and follow-up checks.

1. **Clarify the learning target.**
   - Write what students should learn (content), what they should be able to do (observable behavior), and what acceptable performance looks like.
   - **完成标准**: the target can be observed or demonstrated; complexity is not being substituted with task difficulty or percent correct.

2. **Name the decision and choose evidence.**
   - Decide whether the evidence is for ongoing learning, diagnosis, remediation, enrichment, reporting, certification, placement, or program judgment. Choose tests, observations, products, demonstrations, projects, interviews, journals, or portfolios according to the target.
   - **判停**: if the construct or intended use is unclear, do not choose an instrument yet.

3. **Run the formative loop when learning is ongoing.**
   - Collect a brief diagnostic/formative sample; identify mastered and unmastered goals; give targeted feedback and corrective work; give enrichment to students already demonstrating mastery; use a parallel reassessment.
   - **完成标准**: the evidence changes the next instructional action and a second check shows whether the response worked.

4. **Use the common-assessment team cycle when collaboration is needed.**
   - Teachers align to shared standards, co-develop an assessment, administer it at comparable times, score consistently, analyze item/criterion patterns across classes, share effective practice, revise weak instruction, and escalate shared problems for additional support.
   - **完成标准**: the team produces an analysis table and named instructional responses, not merely a shared test.

5. **Classify assessment purpose.**
   - Distinguish formative from summative by intended use first, then coverage and generalization; use completion time only as a supporting clue. Keep formative evidence free of grading pressure when feedback and correction are the purpose.
   - **完成标准**: the label predicts what will happen with the result.

6. **Write the grading-purpose charter.**
   - Answer: What do grades mean? What evidence counts? Who is the primary audience? What should the communication accomplish? How will the information be used? Keep learning evidence separate from effort, behavior, attendance, and relative rank unless a separately labeled purpose is explicitly required.
   - **完成标准**: the purpose is written before changing gradebook mechanics, scales, or report-card fields.

7. **Design standards-based reporting and family feedback.**
   - Align report fields to standards-based evidence; explain the meaning and use in plain language; share examples; invite questions/comments through accessible and translated channels; assign a response owner; pilot and revise from feedback.
   - **完成标准**: families can identify what was learned, what remains, and how to ask for clarification.

8. **Classify inclusive supports before grading.**
   - State the assessed target. If the support only provides access without changing the target, classify accommodation. If it changes the target, standard, or construct, classify modification; set achievable criteria in the appropriate plan, grade against that ruler, and record/report the basis.
   - **判停**: for legal, course-credit, transcript, or eligibility decisions, consult current local policy and qualified specialists.

## B — 边界 (Boundary) ★

### 不要在以下情况使用此 skill

- The request is only to produce a score, grade, report-card template, or test without clarifying the learning target and purpose.
- The user needs current legal advice, psychometric standard setting, automated-scoring validation, privacy/security compliance, or a causal-effect study.
- The context is outside education and the user expects direct transfer of U.S. K–12 policy conclusions.
- A formative assessment is being used only for ranking or grading and the requester will not provide time/resources for feedback and correction.

### 作者在书中警告的失败模式

- Do not let terminology debates replace a clear student performance goal.
- Do not equate performance level with percent correct or use a curve as a mastery standard.
- Do not collect formative data without feedback, corrective action, enrichment, and reassessment.
- Do not mix effort, behavior, attendance, or relative rank into a learning grade without making the meaning explicit.
- Do not treat a new report-card format as standards-based reform when purpose and evidence remain unchanged.
- Do not confuse access accommodations with modifications; the grade basis and legal/policy implications differ.

### 作者的盲点 / 时代局限

- The 2013 U.S. K–12 context predates modern digital/AI assessment, data governance, and remote-learning conditions.
- The framework needs additional participatory equity analysis for student/family voice, culture, race, language, disability, and power.
- The short-answer format clarifies concepts but cannot replace current law, measurement expertise, or a full implementation plan.

### 容易混淆的邻近方法论

- Formative assessment is a use-and-response cycle, not merely a short quiz.
- Standards-based grading is not a cosmetic report-card redesign.
- Accommodation changes access; modification changes the measured target.
- This skill defines education practice and evidence; `professional-development-evaluation` judges whether a PD program successfully teaches or changes that practice.
