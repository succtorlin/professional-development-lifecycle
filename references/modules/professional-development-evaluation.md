# Professional Development Evaluation

## R — 原文 (Reading)

> “Evaluation should begin during the earliest stages of professional development planning and continue throughout all phases of program implementation, follow-up, and institutionalization.”
>
> — Thomas R. Guskey, *Evaluating Professional Development*, Chapter 9, p. 367

> “1. Participants’ reactions 2. Participants’ learning 3. Organization support and change 4. Participants’ use of new knowledge and skills 5. Student learning outcomes.”
>
> — Guskey, Chapter 3, pp. 114–121

## I — 方法论骨架 (Interpretation)

Professional-development evaluation is a decision process, not a post-event questionnaire.
Begin by defining a worthwhile change in educator practice and the student or organizational result it is meant to support.
Clarify who needs the evidence and what decision it will inform.
Before implementation, test the local context, resources, research base, and feasibility.
Then plan evidence across five distinct levels: participant reactions, participant learning, organizational support, use in practice, and student outcomes.
Use planning evidence to improve the design, formative evidence to correct implementation, and summative evidence to judge future direction.
Choose measures and timing that fit each goal; use multiple sources and meaningful contrasts when possible.
Interpret later outcomes with explicit limits: earlier-level success does not prove later-level causality.
Finish with an audience-appropriate report containing findings, limitations, and specific continue/expand/modify/stop recommendations.

## A1 — 应用示例 (Application Example)

### 示例 1: Kentucky professional-development documentation problem

- **类型与来源**: 原书转述的评价问题；Chapter 1, pp. 40–41; Chapter 2, pp. 90–91。
- **问题/输入**: District records list topics, time, participants, and credit-hours, but do not state intended practice or student results.
- **方法论的使用**: Replace activity-first reporting with explicit goals, goal value, context analysis, success indicators, and evidence at the relevant five levels.
- **结论**: The records are documentation, not an evaluation of merit or worth.
- **结果**: 原书未报告一个统一的后续结果；它用此问题说明必须重做评价设计。

### 示例 2: 合成演练（原书未提供统一的端到端 worked example）

- **问题/输入**: A university offers a semester-long faculty learning community intended to increase use of formative assessment and improve student reasoning.
- **方法论的使用**: (1) define observable practice and student goals; (2) check time, leadership, assessment-policy, and collaboration conditions; (3) collect reaction evidence after sessions, a pre/post learning demonstration, an organizational-support audit, repeated classroom-use evidence, and aligned student work; (4) compare baseline and follow-up where feasible; (5) report findings by audience and qualify causal claims.
- **结论**: The output is a five-level evaluation matrix plus a decision-ready report, not one satisfaction score.
- **结果**: This is a rule-based walkthrough, not evidence that the fictional program works.

## A2 — 触发场景 (Future Trigger) ★

### 用户会在什么情境下需要这个 skill?

1. A school, district, university, or training team is planning how to evaluate a professional-development program before launch.
2. A leader asks whether a workshop or teacher-learning initiative changed knowledge, practice, organizational conditions, or student outcomes.
3. A team has survey results or attendance records and needs a defensible formative, summative, or impact report.
4. Decision makers need to compare professional-development options, costs, continuation, expansion, modification, or discontinuation.

### 语言信号 (用户的话里出现这些就应激活)

- “How should we evaluate this professional development / PD / training?”
- “Did the teacher learning change classroom practice or student outcomes?”
- “We only have satisfaction surveys/attendance—what evidence are we missing?”
- “帮我做专业发展评估 / 培训效果评估 / 组织支持审计 / 学生结果证据计划。”

### 与相邻 skill 的区分

- 与通用 survey-design skill 的区别: this skill decides what to evaluate, why, across which levels, and how results support decisions; a survey skill only designs an instrument after the evaluation question is settled.
- 与 causal-inference or experimental-design skill 的区别: this skill provides practical evidence and attribution boundaries; it does not replace a rigorous causal-identification plan.
- 与 project-management or implementation skill 的区别: this skill evaluates the professional-development intervention and its conditions; it does not manage the intervention itself.

## E — 可执行步骤 (Execution)

**必需输入**: professional-development description; intended educator-practice and student/organizational goals; target participants; context; stakeholders; decision to be made; available baseline, comparison, timing, and resources. If goals, audience, decision, or context are missing, ask before designing measures.

**交付物与完成标准**: a goal-and-decision brief; context/potential preflight; five-level evidence matrix with indicators, sources, owners, timing, and limits; formative/summative review schedule; analysis and quality checks; stakeholder-specific report with findings, limitations, and action recommendation. No generic score or universal threshold may be invented.

1. **Clarify the evaluation contract.**
   - Write the intended educator practice, student/organizational result, why the goal is worthwhile, the audience, and the decision (improve, continue, expand, modify, or stop).
   - **Complete when** each goal is observable enough to map to an indicator and each stakeholder has a decision use.

2. **Run the context and potential preflight.**
   - Check baseline conditions, policies, time, resources, leadership, collaboration, implementation barriers, research transferability, opportunity cost, and participant safety.
   - **判停**: if the goal is not worthwhile, the context blocks the proposed change, or evidence is too weak to justify proceeding, recommend redesign, deferment, or a smaller pilot before full evaluation.

3. **Build the evidence strategy.**
   - For each goal, select relevant levels and define indicator, data source, collector, timing, benchmark, analysis, and decision use. Prefer multiple quantitative and qualitative sources; add pre/post, comparison, or time-series contrasts when feasible.
   - **Complete when** every intended claim has a named evidence source and a plan for unintended effects, subgroup interpretation, privacy, and limitations.

4. **Assess Levels 1–2: reactions and learning.**
   - Gather content/process/context reactions after the experience, then assess cognitive, psychomotor, and affective learning with goal-matched demonstrations, tests, portfolios, reflections, or simulations; use baseline/pre-post when needed.
   - **Complete when** results say what participants experienced and learned, without claiming practice or student impact.

5. **Assess Levels 3–4: support and use.**
   - Audit policies, resources, time, leadership, collaboration, experimentation safety, recognition, and follow-up. Then assess amount and quality of use after sufficient time, using observation, artifacts, logs, interviews, journals, or multiple perspectives at more than one interval.
   - **Complete when** non-use or uneven use has a contextual explanation and an assigned support action, not just a participant blame label.

6. **Assess Level 5 and attribution limits.**
   - Measure student cognitive, affective, and psychomotor outcomes aligned to goals; inspect relevant subgroups and unintended positive/negative effects; triangulate measures and use meaningful contrasts where possible.
   - **判停**: if timing, validity, baseline, comparison, or alternative explanations are inadequate, report “evidence consistent with” rather than causal proof and state what additional design is needed.

7. **Run quality review and the improvement cycle.**
   - Check utility, feasibility, propriety, accuracy, validity, reliability, systematic analysis, impartial reporting, confidentiality, accessibility, and disclosure of limitations. Use planning and formative findings to revise the program while it is active; reserve summative judgment for an appropriate outcome window.
   - **Complete when** every material finding has a confidence/limitation note and a proposed action owner/date.

8. **Report for decisions.**
   - Organize a brief but thorough report: purpose and goals; context and design; evidence and methods; results by level; unintended effects; limitations; costs/relative alternatives when relevant; conclusion; specific continue/expand/modify/stop recommendations.
   - **Complete when** teachers, implementers, and decision makers can each find the evidence and action relevant to them.

## B — 边界 (Boundary) ★

### 不要在以下情况使用此 skill

- The task is only to count attendance, credit-hours, workshops, or budget expenditure; that is documentation until merit/worth and outcomes are evaluated.
- The user needs a validated psychometric instrument, a randomized causal study, legal compliance opinion, or current privacy/accessibility policy; use a specialist method in addition to this framework.
- The request asks for a guaranteed ROI, a universal score threshold, or a causal claim from a single post-event satisfaction survey.
- The source context is outside education and the user expects automatic transfer; adapt goals, stakeholders, measures, and ethics explicitly.

### 作者在书中警告的失败模式

- Do not stop at immediate reactions; satisfaction does not establish learning, use, or student impact.
- Do not run an evaluation so briefly that participants and students have no time to adapt or show outcomes.
- Do not assume more knowledge guarantees use; organizational support, time, policy, and leadership matter.
- Do not treat activity documentation as evaluation or confuse correlation with proof of causation.
- Do not use a one-shot fad workshop as a substitute for sustained, job-embedded professional development.

### 作者的盲点 / 时代局限

- The 2000 K–12 focus predates modern digital learning analytics, remote work, algorithmic bias, and current privacy/data-governance expectations.
- The framework under-specifies power, equity, participant consent, student voice, teacher workload, and culturally responsive definitions of success.
- It provides practical validity/reliability guidance, not a full measurement or causal-inference protocol; local specialists may be necessary.

### 容易混淆的邻近方法论

- A reaction survey is one input to Level 1, not the whole evaluation.
- Kirkpatrick-like levels are a taxonomy and diagnostic sequence, not an automatic causal chain.
- Program logic, implementation fidelity, cost-effectiveness, and causal inference can compose with this skill but are not interchangeable with it.
